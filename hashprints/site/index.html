01 · About

A print shop that runs on work.

HashPrints is an experimental collection of 3,333 generative pixel prints on Ethereum. Nothing is pre-rendered and there's no reveal. Every print's art comes from a keccak256 hash that the minter had to grind for in their own browser.

To mint (we call it pressing), your browser searches for a nonce that makes keccak256(contract, your wallet, nonce) start with enough zero bits. You then submit that nonce with a small ETH payment. The contract checks the work, prices it with a cooling Dutch auction, and stores the hash forever as your print's seed.

02 · Concept

The hash is the plate.

HashPrints borrows the language of printmaking. In a print shop you carve a plate, ink it, and press it under weight. Here the plate is a hash, the weight is computation, and the press is a smart contract.

PLATE

Your hash

Every nonce you try produces a new 256-bit hash. The one you submit is carved into the chain as your print's seed. It decides every line, ink and glitch, deterministically.

PRESSURE

Work

A valid plate needs at least 16 leading zero bits. That takes about 65,000 tries on average. Each extra zero bit you find is printed as a tick mark across the top of the print.

RUNS

333 at a time

The edition is pressed in 10 runs of 333. Each run opens hot at 4× the floor and cools to the floor over 33 minutes. Every two runs the press gets one bit harder.

GOLD LEAF

Rarity is earned

Nothing about rarity is a dice roll you can't influence. Find 4 extra zero bits and the print is struck in gold leaf. Find 8 and it's holo foil. Extra work also cuts your price.

REGISTRATION

Honest imperfection

Like a risograph, the second ink drum can drift a pixel off register. Where the two inks overlap they overprint into a third colour, and sometimes a row slips in the press.

COLOPHON

Signed in its own hash

Along the bottom margin every print carries a barcode of its own hash bits, the way a printer marks a sheet. You can read the proof straight off the art.

03 · The Art

Anatomy of a print.

Each HashPrint is a 32 × 32 pixel plate traced by pixel walkers seeded from the hash. Two inks, one key colour, a paper stock, and everything in the margins means something.

1 2 3 4 5
  1. Work ticks. One mark per zero bit in the hash. Faint marks show the required difficulty, and bright marks show extra work beyond it.
  2. The plate. A 28 × 26 area where the press draws: lines walked, shattered, dripped, woven or bloomed from the hash.
  3. Overprint & registration. Ink B is printed on a second pass and may drift off register. Where the inks cross, they mix into an overprint colour.
  4. Colophon. 28 bits of the hash, right after its zero run, printed as a barcode in the bottom margin.
  5. Stock. The paper (or night) stock with grain. The 2px margin frames the plate like a real print.

Two editions

The hash picks the stock: about 60% of prints come out Paper (white stock, risograph inks) and 40% Night (black stock, neon inks).

Paper

Cream stock, risograph soy inks, multiply overprint. Ten inks, from Fluorescent Pink to Federal Blue.

Night

Black stock, neon inks, screen overprint. Eight inks, from Acid to Ultra.

Six presses

The press decides how the lines move. Symmetry (none, mirror, quad or rotational) is chosen separately, so any press can come out ordered or wild.

Rarity

Rarity depends on how many zero bits you find beyond what the current run requires. If you stop at your first valid proof, the odds are below. If you keep grinding, you can earn gold.

04 · The Mint

Grind. Press. Pull.

The mint combines two mechanisms. Proof-of-grind decides whether you can press and what the print looks like. A cooling Dutch auction decides what you pay. Extra work cuts through the auction.

01

Grind

Your browser tries nonce after nonce until keccak256(contract, wallet, nonce) starts with enough zero bits (16 to 20, depending on the run). The proof is bound to your wallet, so nobody can steal it from the mempool.

02

Press

Submit the nonce with ETH. Each run opens at 4× the floor and cools linearly to 0.000315 ETH over 33 minutes. Every extra zero bit halves the premium, so you can wait for the price to cool or grind your way down to it.

03

Pull

The contract verifies the work, refunds any overpayment, mints the ERC-721, and stores the hash as the print's permanent seed. Max two per wallet. When a run sells out, the next one opens hot.

05 · Contract

On-chain, in the open.

The contract is a standard OpenZeppelin ERC-721 with the press built in. Its tests check that the website's grind and price maths match the contract bit for bit.

Not deployed, not audited. The contract is written and tested but has not been deployed to mainnet or audited. Everything on this page runs in simulation. If it goes live, the address will appear here.

Press interface

press(uint256 nonce) payable
  → requires keccak256(abi.encode(
      address(this), msg.sender, nonce))
    to have ≥ difficulty() leading zero bits
  → price = priceFor(extraBits)
  → refunds overpayment, mints, stores seed

currentPrice()    priceFor(0)

06 · FAQ

Questions.

Is the mint live?

Not yet. The contract is written and tested but not deployed or audited. The simulator above uses the same grind and price maths, so you can try the full experience without spending anything.

Why 0.000315 ETH?

The target price is $0.80. At ETH ≈ $2,540 (October 12, 2026) that works out to 0.000315 ETH. The price is fixed in ETH once the press opens, so its dollar value moves with the market.

What about gas?

A press uses about 198,000 gas. At 0.3 gwei that's about $0.15, and at 1 gwei about $0.50. When the network is busy, gas can cost more than the print itself.

Why max two per wallet?

So the edition spreads out. It isn't sybil-proof (nothing on-chain is), but each extra wallet needs its own grind, because proofs are bound to the address that ground them.

Can bots or GPUs grind faster?

Yes, and that's part of the experiment. Work works like a currency: someone with a fast machine can grind gold or skip the Dutch premium. For everyone else, waiting does the same job. A typical laptop finds a valid 16-bit proof in under a second and a Gold Leaf proof in a few seconds.

Can I choose what my print looks like?

Sort of. Every nonce gives a different hash and so a different print. You can keep grinding until a valid plate appears that you like, but you can't steer it. And if the run fills up while you're picky, the next run is harder.

What is stored on-chain?

Each token's 32-byte seed and the difficulty it was pressed at. The art is rendered deterministically from the seed by the open-source renderer (hashprints.js), so anyone can redraw any print from chain data alone.

What are artist proofs?

Tokens #1–3 are reserved for the artist. They're free but still need a valid 16-bit proof. Public prints start at #4.
