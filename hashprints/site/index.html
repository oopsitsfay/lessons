About

What this is

HashPrints is a 3,333-piece generative NFT collection. There is no pre-generated art and no reveal. Each print is rendered from a keccak256 hash that the minter finds by brute force in their own browser.

Minting takes two inputs: a nonce for which keccak256(contract, wallet, nonce) starts with enough zero bits, and the mint price. The contract checks the hash, stores it as the token's seed, and mints. The renderer is open source, so anyone can redraw any token from its on-chain seed.

Minting

How minting works

Every mint costs the same flat price. The work you put in decides whether you can mint and how rare the result is. It never changes the price.

1

Find a hash

Your browser tries nonces until keccak256(contract, wallet, nonce) starts with the required number of zero bits. Your wallet address is part of the input, so a nonce you find only works for you.

2

Mint

Send the nonce with exactly 0.000315 ETH. You pay network gas on top, at whatever the rate is when you mint. The contract rejects any other amount.

3

Get the print

The contract checks the hash, saves it as the token's seed and mints the ERC-721. Each wallet can mint up to 2.

Runs and difficulty

The 3,330 public prints are minted in 10 runs of 333. The required difficulty starts at 16 zero bits and goes up by one every two runs, ending at 20. Each extra bit doubles the average number of hashes needed.

Times assume about 150,000 hashes per second, which is roughly what a laptop browser does.

Rarity

If your hash has more zero bits than the run requires, the print is rarer. You can stop at the first valid hash or keep searching. The odds below are for stopping at the first valid hash.

Art

The art

Each print is a 32 × 32 pixel image drawn by a seeded random walk over the grid. The seed is the token's hash, so the same hash always gives the same image. The look borrows from risograph printing: two inks, slight misregistration, and a colour where the inks overlap.

1 2 3 4 5
  1. Work marks. One mark per leading zero bit in the hash. Dim marks are the required bits, and bright marks are extra bits.
  2. Drawing area. 28 × 26 px where the lines are drawn.
  3. Overlap and drift. The second ink can be offset by a pixel. Where the two inks overlap, they mix into a third colour.
  4. Hash strip. 28 bits of the hash, printed as a barcode.
  5. Background. Paper or Night stock with a light grain, plus a 2px margin.

Editions

The hash decides the edition. About 60% of prints are Paper (light background, risograph ink colours) and 40% are Night (dark background, neon inks).

Paper

Light background. 10 ink colours, mixed by multiplying where they overlap.

Night

Dark background. 8 neon ink colours, mixed by screening where they overlap.

Styles

There are six drawing styles. Symmetry (none, mirror, quad or rotational) is picked separately.

FAQ

FAQ

How much does it cost?

0.000315 ETH per mint, which was about $0.80 when the price was set (ETH ≈ $2,540, October 12, 2026). Gas is separate and depends on the network at the time you mint. A mint uses about 124,000 gas.

Does finding more zero bits make it cheaper?

No. Everyone pays the same price. Extra zero bits only affect rarity.

Why 2 per wallet?

To spread the collection across more holders. It's per address, so it can't stop someone using several wallets, but each wallet needs its own hash search.

Can a GPU find hashes faster?

Yes. Faster hardware finds valid hashes and rare ones quicker. A laptop browser finds a 16-bit hash in under a second, and a Gold Leaf one in a few seconds.

Can I pick what my print looks like?

Not directly. Each nonce gives a different hash and a different image. You can keep searching until a valid one you like turns up.

What's stored on-chain?

Each token's 32-byte seed. The difficulty is worked out from the token id. Images and traits are rendered from the seed by hashprints.js.

How do marketplaces get the image?

tokenURI points to /api/meta/<id>?seed=…&d=… on this site, with the seed taken from the contract. The API renders the traits and an SVG from the seed. It doesn't read the chain or use a database.

What are artist proofs?

Tokens #1–3, reserved for the artist. They're free, but they still need a valid 16-bit hash. Public mints start at #4.
